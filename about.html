The method

About this book

One shelf holds a physics course; another holds a computing book. The claim of The Bridge is that they are the same shelf, walked in a different order.

The two ends of every crossing

The course side is Landau & Lifshitz, Quantum Mechanics (Non-relativistic Theory), Volume 3 of the Course of Theoretical Physics. It is the same tradition featured in the sibling collection, The Quantum Quartet, and it is chosen deliberately: L&L builds quantum mechanics from the superposition principle and the algebra of operators rather than from wave mechanics alone, so its scaffolding already looks like the linear-algebraic language of quantum information.

The result side is Nielsen & Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (the 10th-anniversary edition). Each essay ends on a specific, named object from that book — a theorem, a protocol, an algorithm — and cites the section where it lives.

The four moves

Every essay has the same shape, so the crossing is always visible:

  1. In the course. The topic as a physics student meets it, in L&L's own framing, with its section numbers.
  2. The step across. The single reinterpretation that turns a statement about physics into a statement about information. This is where the essay earns its title.
  3. The result. The Nielsen & Chuang object, stated precisely, with a short worked example so it is concrete rather than quoted.
  4. Reading. Where to find both ends — L&L § and N&C § — to check the crossing for yourself.

Level and notation

The intended reader has had, or is having, a first serious quantum-mechanics course: comfortable with Hilbert spaces, Hermitian operators, and Dirac notation, but not assumed to have seen any quantum computing. Mathematics is written out — displayed equations render through KaTeX — but every step is motivated. Where L&L and N&C use different notation for the same object (L&L's $\hat{H}$ and N&C's gate matrices, for instance), the essay says so explicitly.

On sources

This book reproduces no copyrighted text from either work. It cites section numbers and states results in original prose and original examples, in the same spirit as its sibling collection. Readers are pointed toward legitimately available copies of both books; the value here is the crossing between them, not a substitute for either.

Status

The scaffold and the full fifteen-essay map are in place. Three essays are written in full — the qubit, no-cloning, and teleportation — chosen to fix the voice across a foundations essay, a theorem essay, and a protocol essay. The remaining twelve are mapped on the contents page and will be filled in.
