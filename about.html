Reader’s guide

How to read this book

Work with the state or probability distribution on paper. Each chapter gives enough intermediate steps to check where its result comes from.

Background and reading order

You should be comfortable with complex conjugation, matrix multiplication, inner products, eigenvalues, and elementary probability. Chapter 1 introduces bras, kets, qubits, and tensor products. Chapter 5 also uses the Schrödinger equation. You do not need prior quantum computing or access to a particular textbook.

Read Chapters 1–3 before working with measurements or reduced states. Chapters 4–5 establish gate conventions and control. Chapters 6–9 develop entanglement and communication; Chapters 10–12 calculate algorithms. Chapters 13–15 cover noise, recovery, and information. Each chapter lists the prerequisites relevant to its calculation.

All fifteen chapters are written, with thirty problems and worked answers. Their length varies with the calculation: a protocol needs its register states, a bound needs its assumptions, and an algorithm needs its output distribution and resource count.

How to check your understanding

After a state transformation, check its norm. After a probability calculation, check positivity and that probabilities sum to one. For density matrices, check trace and eigenvalues. For a protocol, identify which party holds each register and which records they know. For an algorithm, distinguish the probability of an answer from the cost of obtaining it.

The problems change an input, a measurement, or an assumption from the worked example. Their answers are hidden until opened, and use intermediate calculations rather than a verdict alone. If an answer disagrees with yours, first check basis order, conjugation, and which matrix acts first.

Sources and proof boundaries

The main reference is Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac L. Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information, 10th Anniversary Edition (2010). Section pointers were checked against that edition’s contents. Sakurai and Napolitano’s Modern Quantum Mechanics provides further physical background where identified. Primary papers are linked in the relevant chapters.

References support the mathematics and provide longer treatments. The chapters are independent explanations, not a comparison of textbooks. No chapter needs to argue that a physics course missed an idea before introducing it.

The book derives small-system calculations in full and explicitly identifies the larger theorems it uses without proof, including universality, the unstructured-search lower bound, the general recovery criterion, and the Holevo bound. These references are the next reading step if you need those proofs. Source prose and textbook PDFs are not included in the published site.
