# Book2 rework plan: make the explanations easier to reach

Status: APPROVED 18 September 2026 with four process amendments (folded in below). Pilots (chapters 1 and 12)
drafted and self-reviewed; awaiting the joint pilot review.

## What kind of rework this is

Book1 needed a new organising principle. Book2 does not. Its explanations, worked calculations, problems
and limits on claims stay. The rework changes how a reader reaches them: each chapter gets a question, a
figure that helps answer it, a visible main path with optional depth, and a bridge to the next chapter.

**Reader.** Knows complex numbers and linear algebra. Need not know quantum information. May or may not
have read book1.

**Order of work within a chapter.** Question first, then the figure that helps answer it, then the split
between main path and optional depth. A figure that does not serve the question is not added.

## Kept unchanged

- Chapter URLs and file names, the reading order, the fifteen-chapter scope.
- The thirty problems with expandable worked answers (they may gain a wrong-turn note, never lose content).
- The "claims that must retain their qualifications" list in `CONTEXT.md`. It is an editorial safeguard,
  not a proof of correctness: see "Two kinds of checking" below.
- `verify.sh`, `scripts/check_calculations.py`, `check_content.py`, `check_browser.mjs`, vendored KaTeX.
- The conventions in `CONTEXT.md` (basis order, rotation sign, Bell labels, QFT sign, base-two logs).

## Added to every chapter

1. **Opening question** in the lede: concrete, mathematical or operational, answered by the end.
2. **Explanatory figures.** No quota: each figure must explain a distinct relationship, and has a stated
   teaching job. Some chapters will have one, some two. Inline SVG generated by a
   script in `scripts/figures/`, written between `<!-- FIGURE:name -->` markers as in book1. Quantitative
   figures are computed, not drawn by hand. Each works in light, dark, print and at phone width, and
   has a text description.
3. **Reader outcome**: one sentence in the brief ("after this chapter the reader can …"), used as the
   acceptance test for the chapter, not printed as a learning-objectives box.
4. **Main path and optional depth.** The calculation that answers the chapter's question stays visible.
   Supporting generalisations and long algebra move into `<details>` fold-outs. The main path must read
   correctly with every fold-out closed. Qualifications about cost, scope or practical speedup are never
   folded away.
5. **Closing bridge**: a short final paragraph saying what was established and why the next chapter follows.
6. **Honest prerequisites.** The "Before you start" note names what is actually needed, with links to the
   specific earlier section. Later chapters are not advertised as independently accessible: Bell
   inequalities need tensor products, measurement and expectation values, and the note says so.

Chapter length follows the explanation. There is no word target.

## Two kinds of checking

- **Calculation tests** (`check_calculations.py`) confirm that worked numbers and matrix identities are
  right. Testing is proportional to what could be wrong. A numerical figure needs an independent check of
  the calculation behind it (a second method, not the plotting code run twice). A circuit or register
  diagram needs a check of register order, gate order and labels against the chapter's conventions.
  Extend an existing test group where the new material belongs to it; add a group only for new ground.
- **Scientific review** is a separate reading of each reworked chapter for wrong or overbroad statements,
  against Nielsen & Chuang and the qualifications list. Passing the tests does not substitute for it.
  The review record in `docs/rewrite-review.md` keeps stating which of the two a claim has had.

## The two books together

- Book1: understanding quantum mechanics. Book2: using quantum states to process, communicate and
  protect information. Book2 is offered as a recommended continuation; book1 is not compulsory.
- No "read book1 first". Instead, specific refresher links where a reader might need them:

  | Book2 chapter | Refresher in book1 |
  |---|---|
  | 01 qubits, 10 interference | Amplitudes and the double slit (`04-quantum-behavior`) |
  | 01, 04 | States, bras, kets, two-state systems (`08-dirac-formalism`) |
  | 02 measurement, 13 channels | Measurement and decoherence (`14-measurement`) |
  | 05 rotations | Spin ½ and the Pauli matrices (`11-spin`) |
  | 06, 07 | Product states and identical particles (`12-identical-particles`) |

- Notation is coordinated, but useful domain distinctions stay. Book2 reserves $H$ for the Hadamard gate
  and writes $\mathcal H$ for the Hamiltonian; book1 writes $H$ for the Hamiltonian. The notation guide
  and chapter 5 state that translation explicitly, along with $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$ against book1's spin
  up and down.

## Provisional questions and figures

Pilot rows are firm. The others are starting points, revised when each chapter is briefed.

| Ch | Opening question | Figure's teaching job |
|---|---|---|
| 01 qubits | How can two preparations give identical measurement statistics yet be different states? | Same state, two measurement bases: the $Z$ statistics agree, the $X$ statistics reveal the relative phase. Bloch sphere as a second figure only if the derivation needs it. |
| 02 measurement | What does a measurement specify beyond the chance of each outcome? | Two apparatuses with the same outcome probabilities and different post-measurement states. |
| 03 density matrices | What can we predict when we know only part of the preparation, or only part of the system? | Two preparations with one density matrix, against $\lvert+\rangle$: which measurements can and cannot tell them apart. |
| 04 gates | What does a circuit diagram compute, and in which order? | A small circuit with the amplitudes tracked wire by wire, tied to the matrix product order. |
| 05 rotations | How long a pulse, about which axis, makes a given gate? | Bloch-sphere trajectory on resonance and detuned: speed and reachable population. |
| 06 no-cloning | Why can a machine copy $\lvert0\rangle$ and $\lvert1\rangle$ but not their superpositions? | What linearity forces the copier to output for $\lvert+\rangle$, beside what a copy would be. |
| 07 Schmidt | How do we tell whether a two-part state factorises, and how entangled is it? | Coefficient matrix to singular values: the worked non-obvious state before and after the Schmidt change of basis. |
| 08 Bell | Which assumptions limit the correlations, and by how much does quantum mechanics exceed them? | The four measurement directions and the CHSH value against angle: classical bound 2, quantum maximum $2\sqrt2$. |
| 09 teleportation | What must Alice send so Bob can recover an unknown state? | The three registers through time: who holds what, what is measured, what is sent, which correction. |
| 10 Deutsch–Jozsa | How can a function value be used without ever being read? | Phases after the oracle, amplitudes after the final Hadamards, for a constant and a balanced function. |
| 11 Fourier sampling | What does a Fourier measurement reveal about a period, and what is still left to do? | The sampling distribution for an exact and an inexact period: sharp peaks against spread peaks. |
| 12 Grover | How can changing a sign make an answer more likely, and why must we stop? | Four-amplitude bars through oracle and diffusion, joined to the two reflections in the plane and the overshoot in $p_k$. |
| 13 channels | What is left of a qubit's state after its environment is discarded? | Bloch ball under amplitude damping and under dephasing: two different deformations. |
| 14 error correction | How can we detect an error without learning the encoded state? | Syndrome information against logical information: which error each parity pair points to, with the amplitudes untouched. |
| 15 Holevo | How much of a message can be recovered from the states that carry it? | Two ensembles with the same average state and different information; $\chi$ for the nonorthogonal encoding. |

## Steps and checkpoints

1. **Agree this plan.** CHECKPOINT.
2. **Pilot: chapter 1.** Tests whether the entrance becomes welcoming without repeating book1. Shared
   styling is built here, for what this chapter actually needs, not designed in advance. Self-review,
   then go straight on.
3. **Pilot: chapter 12.** Extend the styling for what Grover needs, then consolidate the CSS and the
   browser checks (figure label size, overflow, fold-outs in print).
4. **Review the two pilots together.** Tests whether a figure and optional depth improve an already strong
   mathematical explanation. Main path keeps the four-candidate example, the rotation result, the
   stopping rule and the section on what the speedup counts; candidate fold-out is the matrix-entry
   algebra for $D$ and $G$. Comparing an introductory chapter with an algorithm chapter shows whether the
   approach works for both. CHECKPOINT (earlier only if a consequential decision arises), then adjust this
   plan from what the pilots show.
5. **Chapters 2–5**, then **6–9**, then **10–11**, then **13–15**. CHECKPOINT after each group, with a
   continuous read of the group for prerequisites, notation and transitions. Each batch also updates the
   reader-facing navigation, contents entries and prerequisite notes it affects, so links and
   prerequisite descriptions stay accurate throughout.
6. **One continuous read of the whole book**, beyond the batch reviews.
6b. **Site reconciliation** (final branding and overall copy only; links are already current). Contents page with questions, about page (relation to book1, honest
   prerequisites, the two kinds of checking), notation guide with the book1 translation, reciprocal link
   from book1's final essay, `CONTEXT.md`, `docs/HANDOVER.md`, `docs/rewrite-review.md`.
7. **Title.** Deferred until the pilots establish the voice. Working title: "Quantum Information,
   Explained". "The Bridge" names a comparison the book no longer makes.

Work happens on a `rework` branch. Commits and publication only when asked.

## Acceptance for each chapter

- The opening question is answered by the final section, and the bridge explains the next chapter.
- A reader can answer both chapter questions using only the main path, and understand each figure from
  its labels, caption and accompanying explanation.
- The main path reads correctly with all fold-outs closed; no qualification is hidden in one.
- The figure has a stated job, is generated by a script, and passes light, dark, print and phone checks.
- Every symbol is defined before use; prerequisites are named and linked.
- `./verify.sh` passes, including new calculation groups for new numbers and figures.
- A scientific review pass is recorded separately from the tests.
- No self-assessment, no "the course", no reproduced source prose (existing scans).

## Pilot record (18 September 2026)

**Chapter 1.** Question: how can two preparations give identical statistics and still be different states?
Reader can: compute outcome probabilities in two bases, say which phase is observable, place a state on the
Bloch sphere. Added: a three-source opening in words (no notation), the computational-basis definition the
old text used without giving, figure `sources` (three preparations under two measurements), figure `bloch`
(why A and B share 0/1 statistics: both have r_z = 0), one optional fold-out (the matrix products and angle
substitution the text had only asserted), a closing section, a wrong-turn note on problem 2, refresher
links to the companion guide. Nothing removed.

**Chapter 12.** Question: how can changing a sign make an answer more likely, and why must we stop?
Reader can: run one iteration by mean reflection, state the rotation angle, choose the stopping count, say
what the square-root counts. Added: a short statement of the task, figure `amplitudes` (N = 4), figure
`rotation` (N = 8 plane and the overshoot), a three-line angle argument for "two reflections make a
rotation" in the main path, a closing section, prerequisite links to the sections actually used. Moved to
an optional fold-out: the entry-by-entry matrices for D, O_w and G. Unchanged and in the main path: the
four-candidate example, the stopping rule, the multi-target caveat, and the whole section on what the
speedup counts.

**Shared pieces built for the pilots.** `scripts/figures/figlib.py` plus one script per chapter
(`--write` regenerates the SVG between markers); figure and fold-out CSS in `static/style.css`; optional
fold-outs are `<details class="more">` so the "two worked answers per chapter" checks still hold;
`check_browser.mjs` now checks figure label size at phone width, scroll wrappers, captions and
descriptions, and that print opens optional fold-outs; `test_01` and `test_12` check the figure data by a
second method (density matrices; matrix simulation of G).

**Observed while piloting.** (1) Both chapters needed a second figure, each with its own job. Decided at review: no quota;
each figure must explain a distinct relationship. Also decided: Grover's angle argument stays in the
main path with the final matrix visible, and only the entry-by-entry derivation is optional. (2) The geometric argument was shorter than the algebra it replaced in the main
path, so folding away algebra shortened nothing the reader needs. (3) The old text used one term before
defining it (computational basis); expect the same elsewhere and check definitions on every chapter.
(4) The sans face lacks the ket bracket; figure labels containing kets use KaTeX's text face.
