Essays in quantum information

The Bridge

Every result in Quantum Computation and Quantum Information is already latent in an ordinary quantum-mechanics course. These essays make the crossing explicit — each one starts from a topic in a standard course — Landau & Lifshitz, with Sakurai alongside where L&L stops short — and walks it, step by step, to a named result in Nielsen & Chuang.

The premise is a single observation: quantum computing did not add new physics. It took the postulates you already meet in a first serious course — superposition, unitary evolution, measurement, the density matrix — and asked what they let you do once you treat a two-level system as a carrier of information. The no-cloning theorem is the linearity of the Schrödinger equation, read differently. Teleportation is a change of basis on a Bell pair. Shor's algorithm is Fourier analysis on a finite group. This book is a set of such crossings.

Each essay follows the same four moves: In the course (the topic as the course presents it), The step across (the one idea that reinterprets it), The result (the N&C statement, with a worked example), and Reading (section pointers). See About for the method and the editions cited.

Part I · States

The objects of the theory: a qubit, how it is measured, and what its state is — pure when alone, mixed when part of something larger.

  • 01Superposition & the Hilbert space L&L §2 — the superposition principle  →  the qubit, the Bloch sphere, tensor products
  • 02Measurement & the world outside the subspace L&L §7 — projective measurement, and where it stops  →  POVMs and Naimark's dilation theorem
  • 03The density matrix, and the state of a part L&L §14 — the density matrix (the course nearly had it)  →  reduced states & the partial trace

Part II · Dynamics

How a qubit is allowed to evolve — and a hard limit that the linearity of that evolution imposes for free.

  • 04Universality, and the one word that changes L&L §26–31 — the angular-momentum commutator generates rotations  →  discrete universality & Solovay–Kitaev
  • 05Spin, and the gates that are rotations of it L&L Ch VIII — a spin precessing in a field  →  single-qubit gates, Bloch rotations, the Pauli group
  • 06Linearity & the impossible copier the linearity of quantum mechanics  →  the no-cloning theorem

Part III · Entanglement

The resource with no classical shadow: what entanglement is, the paradox that proves it real, and what it buys.

  • 07Entanglement, and the wavefunction that won't factorize L&L — a two-particle ψ(x₁,x₂) that won't separate (a nuisance)  →  entanglement as a resource, and the Schmidt decomposition
  • 08EPR, and the theorem that made it physics EPR 1935 — incompleteness, not error (Sakurai)  →  the CHSH / Bell inequality & its quantum violation
  • 09Moving a state you cannot read state distinguishability & measurement  →  quantum teleportation & superdense coding

Part IV · Interference & algorithms

Superposition stops being a phenomenon and becomes method: the course runs one computation; these essays ask which computations are possible at all.

  • 10Interference, and the myth of trying everything at once Sakurai §2.7, §3.2.3 — two-path interferometry  →  Deutsch & Deutsch–Jozsa: phase kickback & interference
  • 11Bloch waves, and the eigenvalue that becomes an answer Bloch waves diagonalize translations on ℤ_N (L&L §15 + Bloch)  →  the QFT, phase estimation, period-finding
  • 12Search, and the two-level system you build L&L §42, §113 — a driven two-level system (Rabi oscillation)  →  Grover search: a rotation to the target, optimal at √N

Part V · Open systems & information

The course solves particular processes — one decay, one measurement; here quantum information characterizes the whole space of them, and the information they carry.

  • 13Open systems, and everything that can happen to a state L&L §40–43 — the golden rule & spontaneous emission  →  quantum channels, Kraus operators, amplitude damping
  • 14Error correction, and why looking makes errors digital L&L §3–4 — a complete set of commuting observables  →  the code space, syndromes & the nine-qubit code
  • 15Entropy, and the bit a qubit is allowed to carry thermodynamic entropy (L&L Vol 5 — topic only)  →  von Neumann entropy & the Holevo bound
