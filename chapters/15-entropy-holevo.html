Part V · Open systems & information — Essay 15

Entropy, and the bit a qubit is allowed to carry

Two essays in this book contradict each other. Essay 09 proved that one qubit can carry two classical bits. This essay proves it can carry at most one. If both readings sit right, stop and feel the collision — resolving it is the whole point, and the resolver is entropy.

In the courseL&L Vol 5 (Statistical Physics) — thermodynamic entropy $S=-\sum p_i\ln p_i$ (topic only; see note)
→
The resultN&C §11.3, §12.1.1 — von Neumann entropy and the Holevo bound

Move 1 In the course

Entropy enters a physics education as a fact about heat and disorder. In its Gibbs form, the entropy of a system spread over microstates with probabilities $p_i$ is

$$ S = -k_B \sum_i p_i \ln p_i, $$

a measure of how much you do not know about which microstate you actually have. Strip the units and take the logarithm base two — Shannon's move — and the same functional counts that ignorance in bits, $H = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i$. Either way, the course knows entropy as a number attached to a probability distribution: a scalar summary of a spread of classical alternatives.

A note on where this lives. Unlike every earlier essay, the course side here is not in Landau & Lifshitz Volume 3 at all; thermodynamic entropy is the subject of Volume 5, Statistical Physics. That volume is not among the sources this book verifies against, so — by the same rule applied to Sakurai before a copy was in hand — its pointers are given by topic, not section number, and are flagged unverified. The verifiable citations resume on the Nielsen & Chuang side.

Move 2 The step across

The step is the one this book keeps making: replace the classical distribution with a density matrix. The von Neumann entropy

$$ S(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr}\big(\rho\log\rho\big) = -\sum_i \lambda_i \log_2 \lambda_i $$

is nothing but the Gibbs–Shannon entropy evaluated on the eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of the state itself. A pure state has a single eigenvalue $1$ and the rest zero, so $S=0$ — a definite state hides nothing. A mixed state has $S>0$. And the callback is immediate and quantitative: for a bipartite pure state, $S(\rho_A)$ of the reduced state is the entanglement entropy, and the spectrum $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac12\}$ that Essay 07 extracted from the Bell state — the same list Essay 03 built by tracing out a partner — is worth exactly

$$ S(\rho_A) = -\tfrac12\log_2\tfrac12 - \tfrac12\log_2\tfrac12 = 1 \text{ bit}. $$

One ebit of entanglement. Essay 03 produced the reduced state, Essay 07 produced its spectrum, and entropy turns that spectrum into a single number. The same functional, it turns out, also caps how much classical information a quantum carrier can deliver — which is where the collision with Essay 09 gets settled.

Move 3 The result

Suppose Alice draws a classical symbol $x$ with probability $p_x$ and encodes it in a quantum state $\rho_x$, sending the ensemble whose average is $\rho = \sum_x p_x \rho_x$. However cleverly Bob measures — over all POVMs (Essay 02) — the classical information he can extract about $x$ is capped by the Holevo bound (N&C §12.1.1):

$$ I(X{:}Y) \;\le\; \chi \;=\; S(\rho) - \sum_x p_x\, S(\rho_x). $$

Since $\chi \le S(\rho) \le \log_2 d$, a $d$-dimensional carrier yields at most $\log_2 d$ bits; a qubit, at most one. (The bound rests on strong subadditivity of the entropy, N&C §11.4; that is a chapter to prove and we take it as given.) So: one qubit carries at most one classical bit.

The collision, and the accounting that resolves it

But Essay 09 was explicit that superdense coding sends two classical bits down a single transmitted qubit. Both statements are correct, and the reconciliation is pure bookkeeping. Superdense coding does not send a qubit cold; Alice and Bob pre-share an entangled pair, so Bob already holds one qubit before Alice transmits hers. Taken alone, the qubit Alice sends is maximally mixed, $\rho = I/2$, worth exactly the one bit Holevo allows. Bob beats that only because he decodes with both qubits at once — a two-qubit system, whose two bits sit comfortably under $S \le \log_2 4 = 2$.

Count honestly and the paradox evaporates. Two qubits were spent in total: one distributed in advance to build the pair, one sent to carry the message — two qubits, two bits, one bit apiece, and Holevo untouched. The entanglement was the hidden second resource, paid for earlier. This is what the entropy buys the series: an accounting rule under which Essay 09's “two bits per qubit” and this essay's “one bit per qubit” are the same law, read with the shared pair on the books or off them.

And it closes Part V on itself. Essay 13 showed channels that raise a state's entropy and drain its entanglement; Essay 14 showed how to defend against them; here entropy is the meter that says how much was lost and how much a noisy carrier can still deliver. Noise, protection, and the measure of both.

Move 4 Reading

Course side (different volume, unverified pointers). Thermodynamic and statistical entropy is Landau & Lifshitz Volume 5, Statistical Physics — the Gibbs entropy $S=-k_B\sum p_i\ln p_i$ and its information-theoretic descendant. Volume 5 is not in this book's verified sources, so it is cited by topic only; treat the section numbers as absent rather than guessed.

Result side. Nielsen & Chuang §11.3 for the von Neumann entropy, §11.4 for the strong subadditivity that underwrites the bound (stated here, not proved), and §12.1.1 for the Holevo bound itself.

How it connects. This measures what Essay 03 and Essay 07 constructed — the reduced-state spectrum, now a number of ebits — resolves the tension it has with Essay 09, and quantifies the damage done by the channels of Essay 13. Scope, on purpose: we state Holevo and stop at the bound and the entanglement entropy. Optimizing the accessible information, the Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland capacity, and channel capacities generally are a separate essay — the same boundary Lindblad marked in Essay 13.

Check the crossing. Compute $S(I/2) = 1$ bit from the Bell reduced state of Essay 07; confirm $\chi \le \log_2 2 = 1$ for any single-qubit ensemble; then verify that superdense coding's two bits respect Holevo once the pre-shared pair is counted as a spent qubit. The bound is never beaten — only the resources are moved around in time.
