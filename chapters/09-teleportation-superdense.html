Part III · Entanglement — Essay 09

Teleportation & superdense coding

A course teaches that measuring an observable is choosing a basis and reading off a component. Choose the Bell basis instead of the obvious one, and the same act moves a state you are forbidden to read.

In the coursemeasurement as a change of basis; states you cannot read or copy
→
The resultN&C §1.3.7, §2.3 — quantum teleportation & superdense coding

Move 1 In the course

Two ideas from the measurement chapters of Landau & Lifshitz set this up. The first: to measure an observable $\hat A$ is to expand the state in $\hat A$'s eigenbasis and collapse it onto one eigenvector, with the Born probabilities $|c_i|^2$ (L&L §7). A measurement is, mechanically, a choice of basis followed by a projection. Nothing says the basis must be the "natural" one; any complete set of commuting observables defines a legitimate one.

The second idea is the flip side of the same coin, and it is what makes the problem hard. A single measurement returns one eigenvalue, not the amplitudes. You cannot read $\alpha$ and $\beta$ out of one copy of $\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$; and by the no-cloning theorem of Essay 06 you cannot make more copies to improve your estimate. An unknown qubit is, in a strong sense, unreadable and uncopyable. So here is the puzzle a course leaves implicit: how could you ever transmit such a state to a distant colleague, if you may neither read it nor duplicate it?

Move 2 The step across

The step is to pick the right basis to measure in. For two qubits, the natural basis is $\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}$. But there is another complete orthonormal basis of the same four-dimensional space — the Bell basis, the shared eigenbasis of the joint observables $\sigma_z\!\otimes\!\sigma_z$ and $\sigma_x\!\otimes\!\sigma_x$:

$$ |\beta_{00}\rangle = \tfrac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |\beta_{01}\rangle = \tfrac{|01\rangle+|10\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |\beta_{10}\rangle = \tfrac{|00\rangle-|11\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |\beta_{11}\rangle = \tfrac{|01\rangle-|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}. $$

Measuring two qubits in the Bell basis is, by Move 1, a perfectly ordinary quantum measurement — just a change of basis. The entire teleportation protocol is what happens when the two qubits you measure this way are the unknown state and half of a shared entangled pair. The algebra does the rest; there is no new postulate.

Move 3 The result

Quantum teleportation (Bennett et al. 1993; N&C §1.3.7). Alice holds an unknown qubit $|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$ (call it qubit 1). In advance, Alice and Bob each took one half of a shared Bell pair $|\beta_{00}\rangle$ — Alice keeps qubit 2, Bob carries qubit 3 away. The full three-qubit state is

$$ |\psi\rangle_1 \otimes |\beta_{00}\rangle_{23} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\big)\big(|00\rangle + |11\rangle\big). $$

Now re-express qubits 1 and 2 in the Bell basis. This is pure algebra — a change of variables, not a physical step — and it rearranges the same vector into

$$ |\psi\rangle_1 |\beta_{00}\rangle_{23} = \tfrac12\Big[\, |\beta_{00}\rangle_{12}\big(\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\big)_3 + |\beta_{01}\rangle_{12}\big(\alpha|1\rangle + \beta|0\rangle\big)_3 $$

$$ \qquad\qquad + |\beta_{10}\rangle_{12}\big(\alpha|0\rangle - \beta|1\rangle\big)_3 + |\beta_{11}\rangle_{12}\big(\alpha|1\rangle - \beta|0\rangle\big)_3 \,\Big]. $$

Read Bob's qubit in each term: it is $|\psi\rangle$, or $X|\psi\rangle$, or $Z|\psi\rangle$, or $XZ|\psi\rangle$ — the unknown state, acted on by a Pauli that depends only on which Bell state Alice's two qubits are in. So Alice measures qubits 1 and 2 in the Bell basis (Move 2), obtaining two classical bits $a,b$ that name the outcome $|\beta_{ab}\rangle$. The instant she does, Bob's qubit collapses to the matching entry. She phones him the two bits; Bob applies the correction $Z^{a}X^{b}$:

$$ Z^{a}X^{b}\big(X^{b}Z^{a}|\psi\rangle\big) = |\psi\rangle. $$

Bob now holds the exact state Alice started with. Three things are worth pausing on, and each is a course idea in disguise:

  • The original is gone. Alice's measurement collapsed qubits 1 and 2; $|\psi\rangle$ no longer exists on her side. The state was moved, not copied — precisely as no-cloning (Essay 06) demands.
  • Two classical bits are mandatory. Without $a,b$, Bob's qubit is an equal mixture of the four possibilities and carries no information. This is why teleportation sends nothing faster than light: it waits on an ordinary phone call.
  • Alice never learned $\alpha,\beta$. Her outcome $(a,b)$ is uniformly random and independent of the state — she transmits a qubit she could not read, exactly the puzzle from Move 1.

The dual: superdense coding

Run the same resources backwards and you get superdense coding (N&C §2.3). Again Alice and Bob pre-share $|\beta_{00}\rangle$. Now Alice wants to send two classical bits $a,b$ by physically transmitting a single qubit. She applies a Pauli to her half of the pair — this is the reverse of the correction above — mapping the shared state to one of the four Bell states:

$$ Z^{a}X^{b}\otimes I \ : \quad |\beta_{00}\rangle \ \longmapsto\ |\beta_{ab}\rangle. $$

She sends her single qubit to Bob, who now holds both halves and measures them in the Bell basis, reading off $(a,b)$ with certainty. One transmitted qubit has carried two classical bits. The symmetry is the point:

Teleportation spends one entangled pair and two classical bits to move one qubit. Superdense coding spends one entangled pair and one qubit to move two classical bits. They are the same equation solved for different unknowns.

Both protocols are built from a single move made in the course's own vocabulary — measure in a well-chosen basis — applied to a shared entangled pair. The Bell basis is the whole invention; Nielsen & Chuang supply the accounting.

Move 4 Reading

Course side. Landau & Lifshitz, Quantum Mechanics (Vol. 3): §7 for measurement as projection onto an eigenbasis, and Ch VIII (Spin, §54–60) for spin-½; the two-spin singlet and triplet states arise in the exchange-interaction material (§62). A caution on this crossing: L&L's natural basis for two spins is the singlet/triplet basis — the common eigenbasis of total spin $\hat{\mathbf S}^2$ and $\hat S_z$ — and it is not the Bell basis. They share only two of four vectors (the singlet $|\beta_{11}\rangle$ and one triplet $|\beta_{01}\rangle$); the two triplets $|00\rangle,|11\rangle$ are product states, not the entangled combinations $(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The Bell basis proper is the common eigenbasis of $\sigma_z\!\otimes\!\sigma_z$ and $\sigma_x\!\otimes\!\sigma_x$ (verify: their joint eigenvalues $(\pm,\pm)$ label the four Bell states uniquely). Treat that as this book's own construction — closely related to L&L's, deliberately not identical.

For the physics the protocol actually rests on — that entangled halves carry correlations no local description reproduces — the course canon's closest reach is Sakurai, Modern Quantum Mechanics, which treats the EPR pair and Bell's inequality directly. L&L does not: it gives the singlet as a fact of two-electron symmetry, never as a resource. That gap is the honest boundary of this crossing, and the reason the essay's step across — measure in the Bell basis — is the book's, not the textbook's.

Result side. Nielsen & Chuang, §1.3.7 for teleportation and §2.3 for superdense coding. The primary source for teleportation is Bennett, Brassard, Crépeau, Jozsa, Peres & Wootters, Phys. Rev. Lett. 70 (1993) 1895; for superdense coding, Bennett & Wiesner, Phys. Rev. Lett. 69 (1992) 2881.

Check the crossing. Derive the four-term identity yourself: substitute $|0\rangle_1|0\rangle_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|\beta_{00}\rangle + |\beta_{10}\rangle)$ and the analogous expansions into $|\psi\rangle_1|\beta_{00}\rangle_{23}$, then collect terms by Alice's Bell state. Every Pauli correction $Z^aX^b$ appears exactly where the collapse leaves Bob.

One trap worth naming. The overall sign of the singlet $|\beta_{11}\rangle$ is convention-dependent. With $|\beta_{11}\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2$ as defined above, the fourth term is $\alpha|1\rangle-\beta|0\rangle = XZ|\psi\rangle$ exactly; adopt the opposite sign for $|\beta_{11}\rangle$ and you get its negative, $\beta|0\rangle-\alpha|1\rangle = ZX|\psi\rangle$. The two differ only by an unobservable global phase (recall $XZ=-ZX$), and the correction $Z^aX^b$ recovers $|\psi\rangle$ either way. So if your sign disagrees with the display above, check your definition of $|\beta_{11}\rangle$ before you doubt your algebra.
