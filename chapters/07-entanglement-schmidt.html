Part III · Entanglement — Essay 07

Entanglement, and the wavefunction that won't factorize

Bell (08) and teleportation (09), the two essays that follow, both rest on this one concept; here is the concept itself. A course meets it as a computational nuisance. Nielsen & Chuang meet the same fact as the resource that defines the field.

In the courseL&L — a two-particle $\psi(x_1,x_2)$ that will not separate, met as an obstacle
→
The resultN&C §2.5 — entanglement as a resource, and the Schmidt decomposition

Move 1 In the course

The workhorse technique for any multi-particle problem in the course is separation of variables. Faced with a two-particle Schrödinger equation, you guess that the wavefunction factorizes,

$$ \psi(x_1, x_2) = \varphi(x_1)\,\chi(x_2), $$

substitute, and hope the equation splits into two one-particle problems you can already solve. It works precisely when the two particles do not interact — when the Hamiltonian carries no term coupling $x_1$ to $x_2$. The instant an interaction $V(x_1,x_2)$ appears, the trick fails: the equation refuses to separate, and the true solution is a genuinely two-variable function that cannot be written as any product $\varphi(x_1)\chi(x_2)$. The helium atom is the standard casualty; the course reaches for perturbation theory and self-consistent fields precisely because the exact state will not factor.

So the course does meet entanglement — a non-factorizing joint state is exactly what entanglement is — but it meets it wearing the wrong clothes. A state that will not separate is, to a problem-solver, the hard case: the reason the easy method broke, an obstacle to be approximated away. The whole of this essay is the observation that this obstacle is the prize.

Move 2 The step across

Take the identical fact and change only the attitude. A pure state of two subsystems is either a product,

$$ |\Psi\rangle_{AB} = |\psi\rangle_A \otimes |\phi\rangle_B, $$

or it is not; when it is not, N&C call it entangled, and rather than deploring the fact they build a discipline on it. The non-separability that cost the course its favourite method is the thing that lets an entangled pair carry correlations no product state can — the fuel of the Bell violation in Essay 08 and of teleportation in Essay 09. Nothing about the mathematics changed between the course and the computer; the sign flipped on how it is valued. An obstacle became an asset.

Move 3 The result

To make “how entangled” a number, N&C prove the Schmidt decomposition (§2.5): every pure bipartite state can be written

$$ |\Psi\rangle_{AB} = \sum_i \lambda_i\,|i\rangle_A\,|i\rangle_B, \qquad \lambda_i \ge 0,\quad \sum_i \lambda_i^2 = 1, $$

with orthonormal Schmidt bases $\{|i\rangle_A\}$, $\{|i\rangle_B\}$ chosen to match the state. The recipe is pure linear algebra: collect the amplitudes into a coefficient matrix $C = (c_{jk})$ from $|\Psi\rangle = \sum_{jk} c_{jk}|j\rangle_A|k\rangle_B$, and the Schmidt coefficients $\lambda_i$ are simply the singular values of $C$. The number of non-zero $\lambda_i$ — the Schmidt rank — is the rank of $C$, and it is the whole diagnostic: rank 1 means the state is a product means it is not entangled.

This is Essay 03 read backwards

The callback is exact and quantitative. In Essay 03 we traced out $B$ to get the reduced state $\rho_A$. In Schmidt form that trace is immediate:

$$ \rho_A = \operatorname{Tr}_B|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi| = \sum_i \lambda_i^2\,|i\rangle_A\langle i|_A, $$

so the eigenvalues of the reduced density matrix are precisely the squared Schmidt coefficients $\lambda_i^2$ (and $\rho_B$ has the very same spectrum). The two essays measure one thing from two sides: the mixedness of the part (03) and the entanglement of the whole (07) are the same list of numbers $\lambda_i^2$. Take the Bell state $|\beta_{00}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)$: its coefficient matrix is $C = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}I$, singular values $\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, Schmidt rank 2 — entangled — and $\rho_A = \sum_i \lambda_i^2 |i\rangle\langle i| = \tfrac{I}{2}$, the maximally mixed state of Essay 03, eigenvalues $\tfrac12,\tfrac12$. A product like $|0\rangle|{+}\rangle$ has rank 1 and a pure $\rho_A$. What was “the part of a Bell state is blank” in 03 is “the Bell state has Schmidt rank 2” here — one fact, entered from either door.

A caution: the entanglement that isn't a resource

Here is where a reader can go wrong. Identical particles must carry a symmetric or antisymmetric joint state; two fermions, in first-quantized notation, are forced into

$$ |\Psi\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(|a\rangle_1|b\rangle_2 - |b\rangle_1|a\rangle_2\big), $$

which has Schmidt rank 2 and so, by the letter of the definition, “looks maximally entangled.” It is not entanglement in the sense this essay cares about. The subscripts $1$ and $2$ are not physical addresses — identical particles have no individuality, and there is no “particle 1” you can package up and hand to Bob while keeping particle 2 for Alice. The apparent correlation is an artefact of writing indistinguishable particles as though they were labelled. Resource entanglement requires separately addressable parties — Alice's qubit and Bob's qubit, systems you can act on locally and far apart — and antisymmetrization supplies no such split.

The clean way to hold the two ideas apart: symmetrization is a constraint on which states are legal; entanglement is a property of a state you already have. The exchange-antisymmetry of L&L's identical-particles chapter tells you which multi-particle wavefunctions may exist at all; entanglement, as a resource, is about what two agents can do with a state distributed between them. Conflating the two is a common confusion, and Chapter IX is exactly where the course invites it — a distinction that neither a physics text nor a computing text usually draws.

Move 4 Reading

Course side. Separation of variables is the technique throughout — see the central-field problem (L&L §32 onward) for the separable case that works — and the non-factorizing two-particle state is met wherever an interaction spoils it (the two-electron atom). For the caution, Landau & Lifshitz Chapter IX on the identity of particles (§61 indistinguishability, §62 exchange interaction, §63 symmetry with respect to interchange): read the antisymmetric state there as a legality constraint, not as distributable entanglement.

Result side. Nielsen & Chuang §2.5, the Schmidt decomposition and purifications, and §2.2.8 on composite systems for the product-versus-entangled distinction; Bell states are introduced in §1.3.6.

How it connects. This essay is the concept beneath the two entanglement essays that follow — the resource that Bell (08) will show is real and that teleportation (09) will spend. And it is the twin of the density matrix (03): the squared Schmidt coefficients are the reduced-state eigenvalues, so the von Neumann entropy $S(\rho_A)$ of Essay 15 will turn the Schmidt spectrum into a single number of ebits.

Check the crossing. Form the coefficient matrix $C=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}I$ for $|\beta_{00}\rangle$, take its singular values to get $\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$ (Schmidt rank 2), then confirm $\rho_A = CC^\dagger = \tfrac{I}{2}$ so that the reduced-state eigenvalues equal $\lambda_i^2$. Repeat for a product state and watch $C$ drop to rank 1 and $\rho_A$ become pure — the crossing between 03 and 07 in two lines.
