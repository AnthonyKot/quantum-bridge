Part IV · Interference & algorithms — Essay 10

Interference, and the myth of trying everything at once

Hadamard, oracle, Hadamard, measure is a Mach–Zehnder interferometer drawn in gate notation. Deutsch's algorithm is a two-path interference experiment — and understanding it means dismantling the most popular story about how quantum computers work.

Move 1 In the course

The interferometer is a computation the reader has already run. A particle meets a beamsplitter and takes both arms in superposition; a relative phase $\varphi$ builds up between the arms; a second beamsplitter recombines them; and which detector clicks depends on $\varphi$ through a $\cos^2(\varphi/2)$ fringe. Sakurai works exactly this out — the gravity-induced neutron interferometer of §2.7, the $2\pi$-rotation interferometry of §3.2.3 — a phase in one arm sliding the interference pattern across the detectors.

Now redraw it in gate notation. The two arms are the two states of a qubit; a balanced beamsplitter is the Hadamard $H$, which sends $|0\rangle$ to $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$; a relative phase between the arms is a phase gate; the recombining beamsplitter is a second $H$; and the detectors are a computational measurement. The device is Hadamard → phase → Hadamard → measure. That circuit is a Mach–Zehnder interferometer with no change of physics — only of notation. The reader who has computed a two-path fringe pattern has computed the skeleton of a quantum algorithm.

Move 2 The step across

The one new idea is that the phase shifter can be a function. Take a data register $|x\rangle$ and an ancilla prepared in $|{-}\rangle = (|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$, and let the oracle compute $f$ in the reversible way the course would demand, $U_f:|x\rangle|y\rangle \mapsto |x\rangle|y\oplus f(x)\rangle$. On the $|{-}\rangle$ ancilla it does something cleaner than it looks:

$$ U_f\,|x\rangle|{-}\rangle \;=\; |x\rangle\,\frac{|f(x)\rangle - |1\oplus f(x)\rangle}{\sqrt2} \;=\; (-1)^{f(x)}\,|x\rangle|{-}\rangle. $$

The ancilla is returned untouched and the function's value reappears as a phase $(-1)^{f(x)}$ on the $|x\rangle$ branch. This is phase kickback: the oracle that looked like “evaluate $f$ into a register” is really a programmable phase shifter, writing $f$ into the arms of the interferometer. And it is the inversion this series keeps meeting. In the course a phase is given and you predict how the fringes fall; here you write the answer into a phase so that the fringes will fall where it can be read.

Move 3 The result

Deutsch's algorithm (N&C §1.4.3) is the one-qubit interferometer with an oracle in the middle. Start the data qubit in $|0\rangle$ and the ancilla in $|1\rangle$; apply $H$ to each (the ancilla becomes $|{-}\rangle$); apply $U_f$, which by kickback leaves the data qubit in $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big((-1)^{f(0)}|0\rangle + (-1)^{f(1)}|1\rangle\big)$; apply $H$ again and measure. The second Hadamard interferes the two branches, and the outcome is

$$ \text{measure } 0 \iff f(0)=f(1)\ (\text{constant}), \qquad \text{measure } 1 \iff f(0)\ne f(1)\ (\text{balanced}). $$

One query settles a question that classically needs both values of $f$. The Deutsch–Jozsa algorithm (§1.4.4) scales it to $n$ bits: $H^{\otimes n}$, oracle, $H^{\otimes n}$, measure decides whether an $f$ promised to be constant or balanced is which, in a single query, where a deterministic classical algorithm can need $2^{n-1}+1$.

What is actually doing the work — and what is not

Here is the essay's real job, because the popular account is wrong. After the first Hadamard layer the data register is $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^n}}\sum_x |x\rangle$, and the oracle then stamps every one of the $2^n$ values of $f$ into the state at once. This is the image everyone carries: the machine “tries all answers in parallel.” It is true, and by itself it is worthless. Measure the post-oracle state $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^n}}\sum_x (-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle$ and you get one uniformly random $x$ — a single sample, telling you $f$ at one input, exactly what one classical query would give. The exponentially many evaluations are inaccessible; you may read only one draw from them. Quantum parallelism, on its own, buys nothing.

The power is entirely in the second Hadamard layer, which makes the $2^n$ branches interfere. The amplitude landing on the all-zeros outcome is $\tfrac{1}{2^n}\sum_x (-1)^{f(x)}$: for a constant $f$ every term has the same sign and they add to $\pm 1$, so $0^n$ is certain; for a balanced $f$ the $+$ and $-$ terms are equal in number and cancel exactly, so $0^n$ is impossible. The algorithm works by the destructive interference of the wrong-answer branches, not by parallel evaluation. Every quantum speedup is an exercise in arranging phases so that the answers you do not want annihilate — the parallelism is free, and the interference is the whole art.

An honest measure of the separation

The exponential gap — one query against $2^{n-1}+1$ — is a statement about deterministic classical algorithms. A randomized one settles the question in a handful of queries with overwhelming confidence: sample a few inputs at random; two different values prove “balanced” on the spot, and $k$ agreeing values leave “constant” wrong with probability at most $2^{-(k-1)}$. So the separation is real and it is a query-model statement about determinism — not, by itself, evidence of a practical speedup.

Move 4 Reading

Course side. Sakurai & Napolitano, two-path interferometry: the gravity-induced neutron interferometer (§2.7) and the $2\pi$-rotation interferometry (§3.2.3), where a relative phase between the arms shifts the fringes — the physics the gate circuit redraws.

Result side. Nielsen & Chuang §1.4.3 (Deutsch) and §1.4.4 (Deutsch–Jozsa), with §1.4.2 on quantum parallelism — the “evaluate $f$ everywhere” step that Move 3 shows is inert without the closing interference.

Scope, three fences. No Simon's algorithm, no query-complexity formalism, and no complexity classes. The essay stays with one interferometer and one promise problem.

How it connects. The mechanism generalizes: Grover's search (Essay 12) is this same interference of phase-marked branches, only applied repeatedly to drive amplitude onto the answer rather than in a single shot.

Check the crossing. Verify $U_f|x\rangle|{-}\rangle = (-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle|{-}\rangle$; trace $H\,$–$\,U_f\,$–$\,H$ on Deutsch's data qubit and confirm it reads $0$ exactly when $f$ is constant; then measure the post-oracle superposition on its own and watch it yield one useless sample — proof that the final Hadamard, not the parallelism, is where the algorithm lives.
