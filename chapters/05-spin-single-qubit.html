Part II · Dynamics — Essay 05

Spin, and the gates that are rotations of it

A spin precessing in a magnetic field and a single-qubit gate are the same unitary, read with different intent. The Pauli matrices the course uses for spin are the elementary gates; the bit-flip gate is a spin turned half a turn. This essay builds the rotations that Essay 04 took for granted.

Move 1 In the course

The course's spin-½ comes with a triple of operators — the spin components $\hat{\mathbf S} = \tfrac{\hbar}{2}\boldsymbol\sigma$, where $\boldsymbol\sigma = (\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z)$ are the Pauli matrices (L&L Ch VIII, §54–55). Put that spin in a magnetic field $\mathbf B = B\,\hat{\mathbf n}$ and the coupling of its magnetic moment gives a Hamiltonian proportional to $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$,

$$ \hat H = \tfrac{\hbar\omega}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, $$

and the course computes what happens next: the spin precesses about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ at the Larmor frequency $\omega$ (L&L §111–113, spin in a magnetic field). Its time evolution is the unitary

$$ U(t) = e^{-i\hat H t/\hbar} = \exp\!\Big(\!-i\,\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\Big), \qquad \theta = \omega t. $$

That is the whole of the physics: a two-level magnetic moment turns steadily about the field direction, and after a time $t$ it has rotated through $\theta = \omega t$. The course treats this as something that happens to the spin under a field it is subjected to.

Move 2 The step across

The step is to stop suffering the precession and start commanding it. The same unitary $\exp(-i\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$ that the course reads as “the field turned the spin by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$” is, to a quantum computer, a single-qubit gate: a rotation $R_{\hat n}(\theta)$ you apply deliberately by pulsing a field for exactly the right duration. Only the agency changed — evolution you observe becomes an operation you choose. And the course's spin observables become the elementary gates directly: the Pauli matrices are the bit-flip $X=\sigma_x$, the phase-flip $Z=\sigma_z$, and their product $Y=\sigma_y$.

A note on what this essay is not doing, to keep it clear of its neighbour. Essay 04 asked which rotations suffice — how a finite set of them densely generates every unitary. That question is settled there and not reopened here. This essay only says what the rotations are: it builds the single-qubit gates that Essay 04 consumed as given. The two are the two halves of one subject, deliberately kept apart — 04 the sufficiency, 05 the construction.

Move 3 The result

Nielsen & Chuang package the precession as the rotation operators (§2.1.3, §4.2). Expanding the exponential,

$$ R_{\hat n}(\theta) = \exp\!\Big(\!-i\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\Big) = \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,I \;-\; i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma), $$

and the content of §4.2 is that every single-qubit unitary is one of these up to a global phase, $U = e^{i\alpha}R_{\hat n}(\theta)$ — a rigid rotation of the Bloch sphere of Essay 01. The named gates are particular rotations: $X$, $Y$, $Z$ are half-turns about the three axes; $S$ and $T$ are $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ rotations about $\hat z$; and the Hadamard $H = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(X+Z)$ is the half-turn about the diagonal axis $(\hat x + \hat z)/\sqrt2$ that interchanges the $x$ and $z$ directions ($HXH = Z$, $HZH = X$).

A worked example: the bit-flip gate is a half-turn

Take a $\pi$-pulse about $\hat x$ — the field left on just long enough that $\theta = \pi$. Then

$$ R_{\hat x}(\pi) = \cos\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,I - i\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,\sigma_x = -i\,\sigma_x = -i\,X. $$

Up to the global phase $-i$, that is exactly the $X$ gate. The bit flip $|0\rangle \leftrightarrow |1\rangle$ is not a separate piece of machinery bolted onto the qubit; it is the course's spin, precessed half a turn about $\hat x$ and stopped. Every gate in the set is a precession, timed — the crossing could not be more literal.

The Pauli group

The three gates $X,Y,Z$ square to the identity and anticommute in pairs, with $\sigma_x\sigma_y = i\sigma_z$ and its cyclic partners. Under multiplication they generate, with the phases $\{\pm 1, \pm i\}$, a finite group of sixteen elements — the Pauli group on one qubit. It is worth naming because it is the alphabet of a later essay: the check operators of quantum error correction (Essay 14) are products of Paulis, and the commuting sets among them are precisely the complete set of commuting observables that defines a code space. The gates built here from spin are the letters that spell the codes there.

Move 4 Reading

Course side. Landau & Lifshitz Chapter VIII, §54–55, for spin-½ and the Pauli operators, and §111–113 for a spin in a magnetic field — the precession whose unitary is the gate. The magnetic moment is what makes the field a control knob.

Result side. Nielsen & Chuang §2.1.3 for the Pauli matrices and §4.2 for single-qubit operations — the rotation operators $R_{\hat n}(\theta)$ and the statement that they (up to phase) exhaust the single-qubit unitaries.

How it connects. This builds what Essay 04 assumed: the single-qubit rotations whose finite subsets 04 showed to be universal. The division of labour is deliberate — 04 asks which rotations suffice, 05 says what they are — so the two do not re-derive each other. Forward, the Pauli group here is the vocabulary of the stabilizer codes in Essay 14.

Check the crossing. Verify that $R_{\hat n}(\theta) = \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}I - i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$ is a Bloch-sphere rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$; that $R_{\hat x}(\pi) = -iX$, so a half-turn is the bit flip; and that $X,Y,Z$ anticommute and close into the sixteen-element Pauli group.
